This document defines how an authenticated outside process discovers, addresses, commands, observes, awaits, cancels, and cleans up the Bureau resources it may see. It supersedes the earlier framing of the External Control Plane project, in which Bureau would own a transport-neutral native contract and treat A2A as one projection of it. Under the rulings below, A2A 1.0.1 is Bureau's agent-facing contract, Bureau's own guarantees ride optional A2A extensions, and a small native management plane covers what A2A has no vocabulary for.

These follow from the rulings and from the code, so they were decided rather than asked:

- **Wire projections.** This document consumes [COR-1298](https://github.com/stevekinney/corvidae/pull/183)'s handler boundary as given: `createBureauHandler(bureau, options)` over Weft's engine-independent handler, JSON-RPC over HTTP plus one generic subscription route over Server-Sent Events, no REST bindings, and protocol adapters attached as path-prefix `mounts`. The A2A adapter is the primary mount.
- **Disconnects never cancel work.** A2A states that a task's lifecycle is independent of any one stream. A dropped stream or connection leaves the task running, and the caller reattaches with `SubscribeToTask` or `GetTask`. There is no attached mode in v1. The AG-UI mount ends a stream of its own accord in only two cases, and both leave the run running and tell the client to attach again: `RUN_ERROR` `STREAM_DEADLINE` when the stream has been open for `agUi.maxStreamDurationMs`, and `RUN_ERROR` `STREAM_OVERFLOW` when its reader falls behind `agUi.liveBufferSize`. A client treats both as reconnect signals, not failures.
- **One event authority.** Bureau's durable event history, a filter over one Weft `FleetEventFeed`, is the only source of resumable cursors. The in-memory `bureau.events` and `operative.runs.events` feeds are live frames: they feed the A2A and AG-UI streams and are served over the generic Server-Sent Events route, but their cursors do not survive a restart and are not a resumable contract.
- **Errors.** Weft's `FaultCode` gains the categories this contract needs instead of Bureau inventing a second code space. A2A's own error codes apply on the A2A surface.
- **Versioning.** The Weft catalog keeps its `/v1` namespace and each management operation declares a schema version. A2A carries its own protocol version through `A2A-Version`; each extension carries a major version in its URI.
- **The kit builds on the official SDK** (owner ruling, recorded while scoping the kit). `@lostgradient/weft/a2a` depends on `@a2a-js/sdk`, pinned exactly to `1.3.0` and published as an optional peer dependency. It reuses the SDK's types, its transport-agnostic `JsonRpcTransportHandler`, its `A2ARequestHandler`, `TaskStore`, and `PushNotificationStore` interfaces, its Agent Card signing, and its client, rather than defining Weft-owned A2A schemas.
- **No `required` extensions.** Every Bureau extension is optional. A required extension would reject every plain A2A client, which defeats the reason for being A2A-native.
- **Run creation receipts follow COR-424.** The ledger COR-424 is building keys on the authenticated caller, a caller-supplied request identifier, and the operation version. On the A2A surface the request identifier is the opted-in agent's name with the message's `messageId`, so one `messageId` sent to two agents creates two tasks.

## As built

This section says what `createBureauHandler` serves on `main` at `70daa0063`, on 2026-10-23, with the schedule-fire review work (COR-1613) and, from this change, COR-1543 push notifications and the A2A TCK host. `e1bb7730` is the commit the documentation issue was scoped against, and nothing here is described as of that commit. Where it and a later section disagree about what exists, this section is right and the later one is the decision. The [README](../README.md#external-control-plane) has a worked example of the path an outside consumer takes, which runs as `src/external-control-plane-guide.test.ts`.

### What the handler serves

- **Entry point**: `createBureauHandler(bureau, options)` returns `(request: Request) => Promise<Response>`. The options are `a2aAgents`, `authentication`, `unauthenticatedAccess: 'allow'` (either or both), `mounts`, `agUi`, `publicUrl`, `discoveryUrl`, `signing`, and `maxRequestBodyBytes`. Construction throws for a Bureau with no asset service, for one with persistent storage and no explicit `id`, and for `agUi` or `publicUrl` without a durable engine.
- **Management plane**: every operation in the [management operations](#management-operations) table except `bureau.deliveries.*`, plus `weft.system.principal`, `bureau.events`, and `operative.runs.events`. Calls are JSON-RPC at `/jsonrpc`, subscriptions are Server-Sent Events at `/v1/subscriptions/<name>`, and the OpenAPI, OpenRPC, and AsyncAPI documents are generated from the operations served.
- **Authentication**: an API key resolved by `authentication.resolveApiKeyPrincipal`, and nothing else. A static `apiKeys` list, `jwt`, and `mtls` throw at construction. A subject in the reserved `system:` or `anonymous:` namespace is refused with 401. `unauthenticatedAccess: 'allow'` serves every request as `anonymous:local`, holding every Weft scope and every Bureau scope. It has no loopback guard, so the host that binds the port keeps an unauthenticated handler off a public interface. Both together (COR-1904): a request that carries an `X-API-Key` or `Authorization` header is authenticated by `authentication` and answered 401 when it is refused, never served as `anonymous:local`; a request with neither header is `anonymous:local` everywhere except A2A, whose JSON-RPC routes then need a credential, as each Agent Card's `securitySchemes` and `securityRequirements` advertise. The cards stay public.
- **Owner of record and scopes**: an API-key caller owns what it creates as `api-key:<encodeURIComponent(subject)>`, the local caller owns as `anonymous:local`, and the operator scopes and the predicate are the ones under [Authorization](#authorization). A caller who may not see a resource is answered as if it did not exist.
- **Command receipts**: a run-creation request with a `runCreationRequestId` and a `bureau.tasks.respond` call with a `messageId` go through the durable command ledger (`command-ledger.ts`). An exact retry returns the original receipt, and a retry with a different payload under the same identity rejects with `BureauLedgerConflictError`. The [README](../README.md#run-creation-receipts) has the rules and their retention.
- **Mounts**: the AG-UI mount (`agUi`) starts, attaches to, and resumes runs as AG-UI 1.0 streams, and the asset content route `GET /assets/{assetId}/content` is always present. `publicUrl` mounts A2A (COR-903, `src/http/a2a-mount.ts`): each agent that opts in at `<publicUrl>/a2a/<catalog-name>`, with its public Agent Card at `<path>/.well-known/agent-card.json` and, with `signing`, its key at `<path>/.well-known/jwks.json`. `mounts` accepts other adapters. The [README's A2A section](../README.md#a2a) is the consumer's guide to it, with a worked example that runs as `src/a2a-guide.test.ts`.
- **The host**: Interborough runs the handler on loopback with `unauthenticatedAccess: 'allow'`, so every request is `anonymous:local`. Its state directory, `~/.interborough` or `INTERBOROUGH_HOME`, and its provider configuration are described in [Interborough's README](../../../applications/interborough/README.md), and are not repeated here.

### Unsupported behavior and limits

Each limit below is a fact of the code at `d2972c064`. Nothing here is a guarantee the implementation does not provide, and a section that implies one is describing a decision.

- **`bureau.tasks.events` without persistent storage**: a Bureau with no durable event history answers the subscription with the `UnsupportedCapability` fault, whose `data.capability` is `event-history`, before it opens a stream. The task is still read with `bureau.tasks.get`.
- **`bureau.contexts.input` without a durable mailbox**: a Bureau that cannot take input, meaning one with no durable engine or a session identifier longer than 256 bytes, answers with the `Unavailable` fault and `data.reason` `unsupported-capability`. A Bureau with no session store answers `UnsupportedCapability` with `data.capability` `session-store`. An input that a run ends without delivering is cancelled, and the client sends it again.
- **`expectedRevision` holds across processes, except where a queued run starts, and a timer tick can write its own view over another process's change**: `bureau.schedules.pause`, `resume`, `cancel`, and `update` hand `expectedRevision` to Weft, which compares it inside its serialized write and conditions the write on the exact bytes it read, so two processes sharing a store that name the same revision get exactly one winner and one `StaleRevision`. This holds once every engine on the store carries the change: an older engine writes without the condition and can still overwrite a conditioned commit. Boot recovery of a missing timer, the clearing of a schedule's current run, and a timer tick's first write are conditioned the same way, and a tick whose write is lost reads the schedule again and writes again only if it is still active at the revision and next fire the tick left. What that retry writes is the tick's own view of the schedule, including the slot and the queue it computed from what it first read. So a change another process made to the slot or the queue in that window (a run ending there, or a queued run starting there) is overwritten by it, which is also what an unconditioned tick did before. A tick that loses to a pause, an update, a cancel, or another tick writes nothing, but the occurrence it had started has already run, and a schedule it did not record as running is not seen as running when it is resumed. One write has no condition at all: when a schedule queues occurrences (`overlap: 'queue'`) and a run ends, or a tick finds the slot free, the next queued run starts in the same storage batch that records it on the schedule (`startScheduledRun`'s `scheduleStateAfterStart`), and that batch carries no condition on the schedule's bytes. A pause, resume, cancel, or update made on another process between that read and that batch can be overwritten by it, and so can another process's timer tick: the schedule is then left at the fire time that tick had just run and the timer that tick armed names a later one, so the schedule can stop firing until a boot re-arms it. `bureau.schedules.runnow` is conditioned on the bytes it read: if another process changed the schedule meanwhile, the run it started is recorded on the schedule as that process left it (its current run, or its queued manual run, even on a schedule that process just paused), so a run started that way can outlive a pause. The goal and workflow guards (`expectedRevision` on `bureau.goals.cancel`, `expectedHash` on the workflow and agent writes) are a different mechanism and are not part of this limit.
- **A2A**: with `publicUrl`, each agent that opts in is served over A2A 1.0.1 at `<publicUrl>/a2a/<catalog-name>` (COR-903). Its Agent Card is public, and every JSON-RPC request needs the handler's authentication. The card declares `streaming: true` and declares `pushNotifications: true` only when `createBureauHandler` is given `pushNotifications` (Weft's outbox push sender and its outbox) and the Bureau keeps a durable event history, because a push notification is a projection of that history. `GetExtendedAgentCard` answers `UnsupportedOperationError` (`-32004`), and where push is not declared every push-configuration method, and a send carrying `taskPushNotificationConfig`, answers `PushNotificationNotSupportedError` (`-32003`) before anything is admitted. Where it is declared, `Create`, `Get`, `List`, and `Delete` are served over Weft's push store (credentials redacted on reads, scoped to the caller and task), and a configuration embedded in a task-creating or continuing send is installed before the send's binding completes, so an exact retry finds one configuration, and one that conflicts with an existing id is refused before anything is admitted. An install writes the checkpoint first, marked `pending`, then the configuration, then confirms the checkpoint; each pending checkpoint carries its install's id, so only that install can remove it, and installs wait for the start-up recovery, which confirms a pending checkpoint whose configuration exists and removes one whose does not. Each configuration has a persisted checkpoint (`a2a-push-checkpoint.ts`, `recordVersion: 1`, deleted with its configuration) holding the last durable cursor enqueued. The projector (`a2a-push-projector.ts`) is woken by the durable history and by registry changes (a failure that recorded no event is sent from the registry's settled state), retries a failed pump with backoff, stops watching a task with no configuration left, and enqueues each status event after the checkpoint once (a delivery's identity includes the event's cursor), keeps one unsettled delivery per configuration, and, for a checkpoint below the retention floor, enqueues one current `Task` snapshot and advances the checkpoint to the cursor it was read at. A removed task's configurations are deleted through the sender (which stops the configuration's open deliveries and purges its settled ones with `Outbox.purge`), then their checkpoints, and the host drives delivery with `startPushDelivery` on the same outbox. The projector sweeps every checkpoint once at start and then every `pushNotifications.sweepIntervalMs` (a positive integer, default one hour; the timer does not hold the process open and ends with the Bureau), removing the configurations and checkpoints of a task that no longer exists. Still open (COR-1669): the `task.resynced` repair of a failed durable append. On an opted-out agent's tombstone mount (COR-1669), push `Get`, `List`, and `Delete` serve the task's configurations to its owner, `Create` is served for a task that has not ended and answers `UnsupportedOperationError` (`-32004`) for one that has, and configurations that exist keep delivering until the task ends. `bun run --filter=@lostgradient/bureau test:a2a-tck` starts `scripts/a2a-tck-host.ts` (one opted-in `tck-agent`, `unauthenticatedAccess: 'allow'`) and runs Weft's pinned TCK runner against it with `--sut-host`; the message-response test is deselected in `scripts/run-a2a-tck.ts` because every Bureau send creates a task (the file, file-URL, and data artifact tests pass, COR-788). A stream sends the task's current `Task`, then only later events, and ends once the task is terminal; it stays open through `INPUT_REQUIRED`. Where the Bureau keeps a durable event history, the snapshot is folded from the task's retained durable events and the tail subscribes after the last one, so each later event arrives once with its cursor as its SSE `id`; a run starting, which records no durable event, is sent without one. Without a history, the same sequence carries no `id`. Bureau records the result as one artifact when the task completes, so an artifact never precedes `INPUT_REQUIRED`. A reader that falls behind the stream's buffer, or whose next cursor the retention floor overtakes, is closed and reconnects. The card declares the cleanup extension, A2UI's (`v0.9.1`), and durable history where it is kept; the command-receipts extension is declared too: a send that activates it may carry `expectedRevision` and `deadline`, and its `Task` carries the receipt and revision, with a conflicting `messageId` reuse, a stale `expectedRevision`, and an overtaken deadline typed as `-32602` or `-32603` errors with a `bureau` ErrorInfo. A message carries text, file, and data parts (COR-788). A part whose media type the card does not advertise, a raw part with no media type, and a file part as the answer to a wait are `ContentTypeNotSupportedError` (`-32005`), before anything is admitted and before any model call. A file part's `url` must be exactly this server's asset route, `<publicUrl>/assets/<id>/content`, and resolves to a `managed-asset` source under the caller's owner predicate with `bureau.assets:read`; any other address (another origin, a query or fragment, a `data:` or `file:` URL) is `-32602`, because a Bureau never fetches an address a caller names. An asset another owner holds, one never admitted, and one expired, deleted, rejected, quarantined or pending are one `-32602` ("Asset '<id>' was not found."), as is any asset for a credential without `bureau.assets:read`, so no caller learns whether an asset exists. A `raw` part is admitted as the caller's own asset (`bureau.assets:write`, the policy's inline ceiling and allowed media types, the detected type checked against the card) and enters the run as that asset; the task's A2A history records the asset's route in its place and never the bytes. Admission happens before the send's ledger claim, and the claim records the send's authorized projection (the run's content and the history form, with the admitted assets), so a retry of a claim left pending completes from it: it admits nothing and reads no asset again, so an asset that expired in between cannot fail it. A refused send deletes the assets it admitted only when the refusal is the handler's own (nothing took effect) and its claim was really released; any other failure, such as one that may have come after the run or the mailbox record was committed, keeps the claim and its assets for the retry. An asset that admission reused under `duplicatePolicy: 'reuse'` is the caller's earlier one and is never deleted. The conversation row's id is `a2a:<version>:<agent>:<messageId>`, so the same `messageId` sent to two agents is two commands with two rows. A task-starting message with only text starts the run as before; one with files starts it with a user row (id = the `messageId`) through `bureau.tasks.start(agentName, { append: [row] }, { contextId })`, and input to a working task carries the same content as the session input's payload. A data part enters a task as an inline JSON document for an agent that reads documents, and is ignored, as it always was, by one that reads none. The card's `defaultInputModes` are `text/plain` and `application/json`, plus the media types of every modality the agent's backend descriptors take in and the asset policy's `allowedMediaTypes` admits (an image is `image/*`, a document `application/pdf`; none without an asset service); its `defaultOutputModes` are `text/plain` plus the media types of every modality the agent's backend descriptors (`BackendDescriptor.modalities`, read from `readGenerationProfile(agent)`) put out: `image/*`, `audio/*`, `video/*`, `application/pdf` (document), `application/octet-stream` (file), and `application/json` for a document or file output; an agent whose `generate` carries no descriptor is text-only, and a send's `acceptedOutputModes` is matched against the output modes with `type/*` families. Generated media (COR-788) leaves a completed task as file parts, in its status message, its `result` artifact and its recorded history alike, in the order the model produced them: `url` is `<publicUrl>/assets/<id>/content`, with the part's name as `filename` and its media type as `mediaType`, never inline bytes or a credential. A generated `application/json` asset of at most 64 KiB (`MAXIMUM_DATA_PART_BYTES`) that parses is a data part instead, read as the task's owner; on any failure (missing, larger, unparsable, unreadable) it stays a file part and the projection never fails. Assets are immutable, so a task projects the same parts on every read, stream and push. The reply is folded into the task's A2A record (`history`, unchanged shape) when the task settles, on the instance that ran it, so a restart before any read loses nothing; a task that settled elsewhere or whose fold failed is rebuilt from the run's durable `run.completed` event (its `parts`), and only then from the registry's bounded answer text, which is shown but never recorded as the reply. The asset URL is fetched with a credential holding `bureau.assets:read` (a deployment matter). Reasoning and server-tool content have no A2A part and are not projected. A human wait that carries an input schema (COR-1487: an author configures a JSON Schema 2020-12 per signal on Operative's `requestHumanInput` tool through `inputSchemas`, and the schema rides the pending wait, the park event, the persisted tool result, and the review) projects its `INPUT_REQUIRED` status message as a text part (the prompt, and how to answer) followed by one data part per form, whose data is the array of A2UI messages and whose `metadata.mimeType` is `application/a2ui+json` (each wait is its own surface, `input-form:<review id>`, and a repeated wait on a signal opens with a `deleteSurface` for the park before it): a `createSurface` for the vendored v0.9.1 basic catalog and an `updateComponents` holding the form, `string` as `TextField`, `number` and `integer` as `TextField` (`number`), `boolean` as `CheckBox`, `enum` as `ChoicePicker`, `format: date` and `date-time` as `DateTimeInput`, and a `required` entry as the `required` function; a schema using anything else is one long-text `TextField` whose JSON the person types. The message always carries the form, whether or not the request activated the A2UI extension. The answer is a `SendMessage` continuation with one data part, the form's values as a JSON object keyed by property name (`metadata.signalName` names the wait when several are open); it is validated against the original schema, a mismatch, a text answer, or a bare approval is `RequestMalformedError`, and a valid one is delivered as the signal's payload. A fallback form may answer `{"response": "<JSON text>"}`. Declining a form is not part of this contract; cancel the task. A send is idempotent on the caller, the agent, the A2A version's Major.Minor, the `messageId`, and, for a continuation, the task: an exact retry returns the original task, and the same `messageId` with another payload is `-32602`. An agent that has opted out has no card and starts no task, but while any of its tasks is non-terminal or retained, its path keeps a tombstone mount that serves `GetTask`, `SubscribeToTask`, and `CancelTask` for them. A continuation into a context that was deleted and recreated under the same id is refused, because the task ran in an earlier incarnation, and `createBureauHandler` refuses `publicUrl` when `retention.tasks` is shorter than the 24 hours a send's idempotency binding is kept. `bureau.agents.list` carries no Agent Card URL. The handler serves no run-starting operation on the management plane: a host starts a task with `bureau.tasks.start`.
- **Schedule-fire reviews across instances**: a schedule fire's human wait or tool approval is a review of the fire (COR-1596, #383), and COR-1613 (#404) made those reviews the same on every Bureau that shares the stores. A review another instance closes, decided or expired, reaches the instance that holds the fire through the run's shared durable history, and the fire's `bureau.tasks.events` stream ends when the last review closes on any instance. COR-1621 keeps the instances in step without a client stream, COR-1622 orders, retries and bounds the closes, and COR-1620 (#409) settled how a restored approval's skill policy is decided:
  - COR-1621: each Bureau keeps one subscription on the durable history of the fires it holds a review of, whether or not a `bureau.tasks.events` stream is open. A fire joins it when one of its reviews is announced or restored and leaves it when its last review is released, so a Bureau holding none keeps nothing open. A peer's decision or expiry drops the instance's copy, and a re-gate the peer made leaves the review listed at the round the session holds, refreshed. The subscription reacts to an event before it moves past it: a reaction that fails, such as a session that cannot be loaded, is retried and then read again from durable state, and a gap in the history, where retention passed the subscription, reads every followed fire's reviews again from durable state. A subscription that ends on its own is reopened after a wait that doubles with each end in a row, from the session persistence retry delay to five seconds, and starts over once a subscription has stayed up that long or delivered an event, so one that keeps ending is not reopened in a loop. A catch-up read that fails and a re-read that fails are retried with the same backoff until they succeed, so an outage of the shared store delays the instance's view of a peer's closing and does not lose it. A review the session no longer records, and that has not been held here for 30 seconds, reads as closed by a peer in that re-read, and a younger one is left alone because its record may not have landed yet. A decision reads the round from the session before it claims, so a decision on a round a peer has since re-gated is refused with `CONFLICT` and the review is refreshed. The round is read again once the claim is held, because a peer re-gates by writing the next round and only then giving up its claim of the one it left. The `review.canceled` audit record and history event of a review round carry the deduplication key `review.canceled:<review id>:<round>`, so two instances that observe one cancel record each round once. A round is the one the instance reads, so an instance still at a round a peer has re-gated past can record that round's closing beside the peer's record of the next, each once; the key's review id is bounded as the decision record's is, a longer id appearing as a digest. A history event written without the key is read as it always was.
  - COR-1620: a restored approval is decided under the skills the run has active now, read again on each approve. An approval whose skill policy cannot be verified is refused with `CONFLICT` and the reason, and a deny still goes through. Unverifiable is a snapshot that cannot be read or deserialized (an unreadable checkpoint or session), one that names another fire or run (a recurring schedule's newer fire, or a later run that cleared the session's snapshot), a stored skill the catalog refuses on recovery, and a run with no toolbox of its own whose session names skills. A fire replayed after its process died in a step, and a session run reattached the same way, are offered no tools under an unverifiable snapshot and keep the snapshot they hold, and each logs a warning. A recurring fire on a Bureau with skills is deny-only once a newer fire has overwritten its session and the process restarts. A skill's allow list filters the tools a run is offered; it does not gate execution, so the refusal happens at approve.
  - COR-1622: a fire's review history is never left with a request open or a close out of order, and a close that fails is retried. One rule counts a park (Operative's `humanWaitParkSignalNames`: consecutive results on one signal in a step are one park), so the id a checkpoint walk derives and the id a request carries agree. A fire's first request waits for its seed read of the history, and every writer of a close (a decision, an expiry, a revoke, a cancel, a supersession) waits for the same read, so a close always follows its request. At boot each canceled fire's open requests are closed, whether or not its task is settled, and a settlement never waits for a close: the retry list is each request whose current round no decision has ended, together with the human wait the checkpoint ends on (a review's round advances only on a supersession paired with that round's decision, a supersession with no decision after it ends the request, and a request whose only close is a `review.canceled` stays on the list so the audit half of that close is checked), which is read strictly, so a malformed latest step closes nothing and a later boot with a readable checkpoint closes the right review. The audit record and the history event of a close are checked on their own and only the missing one is written, and an audit record written at boot is rebuilt from what the fire's checkpoint committed (a tool approval's `pendingApproval`, a human wait's signal and prompt) and never from the review id: a checkpoint that cannot be read strictly, or that does not describe the review, writes no record and the boot reports it, so a history append that failed after the audit record committed is written at the next boot and the audit record is not written twice. A fire's seed state leaves with the fire's final reconciliation. A Bureau with no maintenance timer of its own (no durable engine, memory or audit retention to drive) arms an expiry sweep every five minutes for as long as it holds a tool approval, counting one whose binding has run out and one being decided, and stops it the moment the last one leaves, and a `'manual'` host's `maintain` runs the sweep. The follower of COR-1621 counts a fire reconciled only when its reviews match durable state: a session a peer deleted closes the approvals held in it, a review too young to verify is read again when its grace ends (a wait of its own, outside what `settled()` waits for), an event or a page record that cannot be decoded reads the fire again from its state, and a closing reaction that unfollows its fire still finishes telling those following the task, with the same backoff and no attempt limit.
- **A peer answers a fire's human wait**: another instance answers a fire's `requestHumanInput` park, through `bureau.tasks.respond` or `reviews.resolve`, by delivering the signal through the engine by run id, without holding the fire, and the fire completes on its holder. A tool approval a peer restored is decided on that peer under the same rules. The answer is fenced by the durable decision record, so two instances deciding one park decide it once.

## What exists today

At `d2972c064`, Bureau has an external surface: `createBureauHandler` (COR-1300) serves it over Weft's engine-independent handler (COR-1299), and Interborough, the first consumer, imports `@lostgradient/bureau` and runs it. [As built](#as-built) describes that surface. The pieces it builds on:

- **Weft's operation catalog.** `defineOperation` operations with zod input, output, and event schemas, an access policy, an optional `authorize` hook, and a transports matrix (`packages/weft/src/server/operation-catalog/types.ts:178-303`). One pipeline, `executeOperation`, serves every transport (`packages/weft/src/server/operation-catalog/pipeline.ts:14-43`), and discovery documents are generated from the registry (`packages/weft/src/server/openapi.ts`, `openrpc.ts`, `asyncapi.ts`).
- **Weft's authentication plumbing.** API keys compared in constant time, JWT, mTLS, a principal with a closed set of 21 scopes (`packages/weft/src/server/authorization-scope.ts`), audit of authentication decisions, redaction, opt-in CORS, and body limits. There is no tenant concept anywhere in Weft.
- **Weft's command mailbox.** Durable FIFO admission with receipts, an idempotency binding over caller, target, kind, and payload digest, and a `conflict` outcome that leaves the original command untouched (`packages/weft/src/core/mailbox-contract.ts`, `mailbox-admission.ts`). Terminal receipts are retired after `terminalRetentionMs`, 24 hours by default, which also releases the idempotency key.
- **Weft's delivery outbox.** At-least-once delivery with `attempting` persisted before the transport call and an explicit `unknown-outcome` state (`packages/weft/src/core/outbox-types.ts:74-115`), plus `drain()`.
- **Weft's fleet event feed.** Opaque decimal cursors, an in-band `fleet:gap` envelope below the retention floor, and a snapshot-then-tail handoff (`packages/weft/src/server/fleet-event-feed.ts`, `workflow-event-feed.ts:255-261`). Bureau's maintenance pass calls `retain()` over events older than `BureauOptions.retention.events`, which is how the floor advances.
- **Bureau's durable event history.** A filter over one fleet feed by owner (`run`, `session`, `schedule`, `goal`), with `page` returning `{ outcome: 'gap', requestedCursor, firstRetainedSequence }` and `subscribeEventHistory` skipping gaps silently (`packages/bureau/src/durable-event-history.ts:355-401`).
- **The started-work floor.** `LivenessSnapshot` (`packages/operative/src/liveness/types.ts:173-235`) and `CleanupAcknowledgement` (`packages/operative/src/types.ts:175-195`, statuses `not-required`, `completed`, `failed`, `unresolved`) give every resource identity, revision, status, ownership, durability, and cleanup evidence. The ownership matrix in [runtime contracts](../../../documentation/runtime-contracts.md#ownership-and-cleanup) is the in-process version of the resource table below.
- **Principal attribution.** Runs, children, goals, and event-history reads carry an optional principal string, session input, steering, and review resolution require one, and a mismatch already answers `not-found` rather than `forbidden`.

## Architecture

Three surfaces sit over one set of native Bureau resources. Each is a projection: it translates its wire format into dispatches against the same catalog operations, the same authorization predicate, and the same durable history, so none of them can disagree about a resource's state.

| Surface | Mounted as | Audience | Wire |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A2A | A `mounts` entry claiming one path prefix per agent that opts in, plus the public Agent Card path | External agents, peer Bureaus, the Interborough's task views | A2A 1.0.1 JSON-RPC binding; streaming over Server-Sent Events |
| AG-UI | A `mounts` entry (COR-855) | The Interborough's chat surface | AG-UI 1.0 event stream |
| Management plane | Catalog operations in the shared Weft registry | Operators, through the Interborough | JSON-RPC over HTTP; subscriptions over the generic Server-Sent Events route |

Adapters reach Bureau only through the mount context's `dispatch` and `subscribe`, never by calling the `bureau` object directly, so every request passes the catalog's access policy and `authorize` hook ([COR-1298, decision 4](https://github.com/stevekinney/corvidae/pull/183)). An adapter that bypasses `dispatch` is a defect.

The protocol mechanics live in Weft and the Bureau semantics live in Bureau. `@lostgradient/weft/a2a` wraps the official `@a2a-js/sdk` in a fetch handler, with streaming over Server-Sent Events, version and extension negotiation, the public Agent Card and its signing keys, durable stores over Weft storage, and outbox-backed push delivery. Bureau implements the SDK's `A2ARequestHandler` over its runs; Weft's reference backend implements the SDK's executor over workflows. See [Changes to Weft](#changes-to-weft).

### Task states

A task is the external face of one agent run. Bureau maps its run and review states onto A2A's task states and keeps the finer detail in the cleanup extension:

| Bureau condition | A2A task state |
| --- | --- |
| Admitted, not yet started | `TASK_STATE_SUBMITTED` |
| Running, aborting, or cleaning up before a terminal result | `TASK_STATE_WORKING` |
| Waiting on a human-wait signal or an elicitation the requester answers | `TASK_STATE_INPUT_REQUIRED` |
| Waiting on an approval the requester decides | `TASK_STATE_INPUT_REQUIRED` |
| Waiting on a credential the requester must grant | `TASK_STATE_AUTH_REQUIRED`, with an out-of-band authorization URL in the status message (A2A 1.0.1 §7.6.1); a message answering it may negotiate or decline but never carries the credential |
| Waiting on a review an operator resolves | `TASK_STATE_WORKING`, with the review identifier in the cleanup extension's status detail |
| Finished with `stop-condition` | `TASK_STATE_COMPLETED` |
| Finished with `elicitation-denied` (the requester declined to answer) | `TASK_STATE_FAILED`, with the finish reason in the status message |
| Finished with `maximum-steps`, `error`, `tripwire`, or `budget-exceeded`, matching Operative's `isFailureFinishReason` (`packages/operative/src/finish-reason.ts`) | `TASK_STATE_FAILED` |
| Finished with `aborted` | `TASK_STATE_CANCELED` |
| Refused at admission | `TASK_STATE_REJECTED` |

`TASK_STATE_CANCELED` is terminal in A2A, but cleanup may still be pending when the result settles. The cleanup extension carries the `CleanupAcknowledgement` separately, so a client that cares can wait for `completed` and one that does not can stop at the terminal state.

### Tasks and contexts in code

A task is one top-level Bureau run. Its identifier is the run identifier, verbatim: `run-<n>-<uuid>`, and there is no second namespace and no mapping table. Child runs and goal attempts are not tasks in v1: each belongs to its own operations, and the `bureau.tasks.*` operations answer one as a missing task. A schedule fire is a task (COR-1482): its identifier is the fire's run identifier, its owner is the schedule's owner, and the scheduler's service principal, `system:scheduler`, is recorded as the task's `actor`, so the owner lists, reads, observes, and cancels its schedule's fires with no operator scope. The fire is an explicit kind of task (`kind: 'schedule-fire'` on its record), not a catalog task. It is in the context lifecycle: while it is unsettled it keeps its context `running` in `bureau.contexts.list` and `get` though it holds no run in Bureau's run store, and `bureau.contexts.delete` cancels it with the context's other live tasks, reporting its stop as unresolved because a headless fire has no cleanup to acknowledge. A fire writes no `run.*` events to the durable history, so `bureau.tasks.events` follows it from the task registry instead, the source `bureau.tasks.get` reads: a `task.snapshot`, then a `task.status` element each time what the task reads as changes while it is not over (`input-required` when a review of the fire opens, and `working` or `input-required` again when a wait is answered or closes), then one `task.status` element carrying the fire's end (`completed` with its answer as an artifact, `failed`, or `canceled`), then the stream ends, whether or not the fire is bound to its session and whether or not the Bureau keeps a durable history. A fire that settled with a review still open ends when its last review closes, decided or expired, and not when it settled. The elements before the end reuse the snapshot's cursor, `-1`, so a client cannot resume from one of them: a reconnect with that cursor gets a fresh stream that is told the fire's current status first, so a status that changed while the client was away is not missed. The end is told by the process that serves the stream: a review another process sharing the store closes reaches it through the run's shared durable history (COR-1613), so the stream ends when the last review closes on any instance. A fire that settled holding a pending review also keeps its context `awaiting-input` in `bureau.contexts.list` and `get`, as the task reads `input-required`. A client that reconnects with that element's cursor has seen the end and gets an empty stream. A fire that times out settles its task as `failed` with the finish reason `timed-out`. The task's context is the fire's session, and it is written before that session exists, so it is bound to the session's incarnation once the fire knows it: when it finds an existing session, or when its first step saves a fresh one. Until then the task is found by `bureau.tasks.list` without a `contextId`; once bound, `list({ contextId })` finds it. A fire's task is settled from the engine's terminal event for its run, and ages out with `retention.tasks` like any task.

A task's `contextId` is its session identifier. Every task view the management plane returns says which it is in `kind` (COR-1689): `schedule-fire` for a schedule's fire, `chat` for a task whose record holds the session incarnation it ran in, and `catalog` for anything else. A task in a Bureau without a session store records no incarnation, so it reads `catalog`. A client that lists other owners' tasks, and lists only its own sessions, links a `chat` task's `contextId` to its session and links nothing from a `catalog` one; `bureau.contexts.get` still decides whether the session opens. The kind is derived from the task's record when the view is built, not stored: a record with the `schedule-fire` kind is a fire, a non-empty incarnation is a chat, and anything else is catalog. A task listing's snapshot stores whole task views, so its key prefix is versioned (`bureau-task-listing:v3:`); a snapshot saved before `kind` (`v1`) or `origin` (`v2`) existed is not read.

Every task view also says where the task came from in `origin` (COR-1867), recorded when the task was admitted, or `null` for a task recorded before origins were kept (no migration: the field is absent in the stored record and reads `null`; it is never guessed from `kind` or the other fields). The origin is a union on `kind`: `chat` (`contextId`, `agentName`, `principal`: a person's message in a chat), `person` (`principal`, `surface`: `web`, `palette`, or `api`; a person who started the task directly), `schedule` (`scheduleId`, `fireId`, and `manual: true` when a person fired it by hand: one fire of a schedule), and `api` (no further fields: a start that did not say where it came from, a run whose original start was lost and that recovery re-admitted, or an A2A peer's message). `bureau.tasks.start` takes `options.origin` with only the kind (and a person's `surface`): the Bureau fills a chat's context, agent, and principal from the authenticated start, so a caller cannot name another principal's chat, and a schedule's fire is the Bureau's own. The AG-UI mount declares `chat`; a start that declares nothing is `api`. The origin is not part of the command a `commandId` identifies, so a retry from another surface replays the original task and its origin. `bureau.tasks.list` takes `origin` (one of the kinds) and returns only the tasks recorded with that kind, applied before paging; a task with no recorded origin matches none. The filter is part of the page token's binding, so a token issued under one `origin` is `InvalidParams` (`page-token-filters`) under another, or under none. The run's Weft workflow carries the tag `origin:<kind>` (a durable run Bureau starts for a task; a schedule's fire, which Weft starts itself, is not tagged). The A2A wire does not carry `origin`. A stored record is readable with any origin object that has a non-empty `kind`: an origin of a kind this release does not know, or one with a field it cannot read (a newer release sharing the storage wrote it), leaves the task settleable, listable, and removable, keeps the value as written, and shows `origin: null` in the view; `bureau.tasks.list`'s `origin` filter compares the stored kind.

`RunRef.runId`, the `${sessionId}:${sequence}` identifier, is not a task identifier and does not relate to one. It belongs to Operative's `SessionHandle`, which Bureau uses only inside a goal's conversation. Two turns in one Bureau session are two tasks started with `bureau.tasks.start`, so they are two tasks with different identifiers and one `contextId`, and `bureau.tasks.list({ contextId })` returns exactly those two.

Bureau keeps one durable record per task, written before the run starts and removed when the run is deleted: the owner, the context and the session incarnation it ran in, the originating user message, and how the run settled and how its cleanup ended. The record is how a settled task keeps its owner and its history across a restart, and it is where a task's own message history lives, apart from the internal session transcript. Deleting a context empties the history of its tasks, so a user's message text does not outlive the context it belonged to; their status and cleanup facts remain. The management plane serves the history to no one in v1: it exists for the A2A `GetTask` that follows.

A context's summary comes from Operative's session summary index, format version 2. Every summary there records `title`, `lastMessagePreview`, and `owner` (the owner recorded in the session's metadata when it was created, the local owner for a session that records none, and none at all for a malformed record, which no owner filter matches), computed when the session is saved. A store holding a version 1 index rebuilds it once from the session bodies, the path that already repairs a corrupt index, so no migration step exists. `list` applies the owner filter to the index before `offset` and `limit`, and reads one body per visited row as its existence check, as before.

The management plane shows a task's state as `submitted`, `working`, `input-required`, `completed`, `failed`, or `canceled`. A task whose run finished successfully while a review is still open is `input-required`, and answering that review is what finishes it; a failed or canceled task is that whatever is open. `rejected` and `auth-required` cannot arise in v1.

### Assets

The asset entries are COR-794's contract as built. An asset is admitted from bounded inline bytes, an upload completion, an authorized URL, a provider file identifier, or an existing asset reference, and admission returns a handle or durable locator immediately when work continues asynchronously. Its `AssetDescriptor` carries identity, monotonic revision, digest, media type, byte length, owner, authorization, provenance, lifecycle state, creation time, expiry, retention, and a general-versus-privileged projection distinction, consistent with `ContentSource`, `Modality`, and `MediaLimits` in `packages/conversationalist/src/multi-modal.ts:214-270`.

A peer's artifact enters as the `a2a-reference` content source, which must carry the peer's `taskId` as well as `agentCardUrl` and `artifactId`, because an artifact identifier is unique only within its task (A2A 1.0.1 §4.1.7); COR-1303 extends `ContentSource` (`packages/conversationalist/src/multi-modal.ts`) accordingly.

Externally, an asset appears as an A2A file part whose `url` is Bureau's authenticated asset route, `GET /assets/{assetId}/content` on `createBureauHandler` (COR-1547). Browser media elements cannot attach the `Authorization` header, so a browser client fetches the route with its token and renders the bytes through a `blob:` object URL that it revokes when the view unmounts; Interborough's renderer does this through a host-supplied asset resolver. Fetching it requires the same credentials as the A2A call and applies the same owner predicate, so the URL is an address, not a credential: it is never a reusable signed URL, and bytes are never inlined after admission. Resolving an asset reference applies the same owner predicate as the task that produced it; a caller who cannot see the task cannot resolve its assets. Asset admission, deletion, and cleanup are started work with targeted abort and a cleanup acknowledgement, exactly as COR-794 specifies. Asset events and audit records never carry raw bytes, secrets, or signed URLs. Inbound file parts, whether bytes or URLs, go through COR-794's admission pipeline, and a peer's artifact enters as the existing `a2a-reference` content source. The management plane serves four asset operations in v1: `bureau.assets.uploads.create`, `.append`, and `.complete`, and `bureau.assets.get`, which reads a descriptor and never the bytes. Deletion and `abortUpload` are not served, so an upload never completed expires. The route answers a known asset that is not `available`, an asset another owner holds, and an asset whose content a provider holds (`provider-fetch`) exactly as it answers an unknown one: 404, with no body that tells them apart. The A2A projection of this content (COR-788) and the AG-UI projection (COR-1547) are described in the [README's A2A](../README.md#a2a) and [AG-UI](../README.md#the-ag-ui-mount) sections; the MCP adapter carries no assets. The [multimodal guide](../README.md#multimodal-content-and-managed-assets) maps every surface and lists what is not supported.

The card's security declaration is generated from the authentication configuration the handler actually enforces, never written by hand, so the two cannot drift (COR-718). The extended card is not declared in v1: it has no consumer, and `capabilities.extendedAgentCard` stays false.

The A2A opt-in is the deployment's choice and description is the agent's. The opt-in decides what A2A serves, and what the management listing shows a caller without `bureau.agents:read` (COR-1904); to a caller holding that scope chat (the AG-UI mount) serves any ready agent and the listing shows every one, while a caller without it starts a new AG-UI run, creates a schedule, or creates a goal only for an agent that opts in, and any other answers as an unknown name does. For a code-defined agent, `createBureauHandler`'s `a2aAgents` allowlist is the only source of the opt-in. For a persisted agent (an agent file, COR-1524), its own `a2a` frontmatter is: the file is the deployment's, it says whether the agent is offered over A2A, and `bureau.agents.definitions.update` changes that one key. The allowlist never names a persisted agent and a file never opts in a code-defined one, so neither source overrides the other. An agent that is neither in the allowlist nor opted in by its file has no card, cannot start new tasks over A2A, and reads as not found on its A2A path. The opt-in gates a new A2A task only: the tombstone rule below keeps the continuation of a task that is waiting for an answer working. An agent file that is invalid is not an agent, so it never opts in. Today `bureau.agents.list` reports the opt-in as `a2a` on every request, and a caller without `bureau.agents:read` sees only the agents that opt in (COR-1904); the A2A mounts (COR-903) read the same state on every request, so opting in or out takes effect without a restart. An agent that opts out and still has any addressable task, non-terminal or terminal within `retention.tasks`, keeps a tombstone mount at its path: its owners can still `GetTask`, `SubscribeToTask`, and `CancelTask` those tasks, continue a task in `INPUT_REQUIRED` or `AUTH_REQUIRED` with a `SendMessage` carrying its `taskId`, `Get`, `List`, and `Delete` their push configurations, and `Create` one for a non-terminal task (with `Create`'s idempotency, so a retry of one committed before the opt-out returns it), while a genuinely new `SendMessage` without a `taskId` returns not-found and no card is served; an exact retry of a creation command whose binding is still retained resolves to its original task and receipt, as the retry-first rule requires. The tombstone ends only when the agent's last task has passed its terminal retention and is no longer addressable, so a client that disconnected before completion can still `GetTask` the final status and artifacts. The card's identity fields come from Bureau's immutable catalog, and its descriptive fields from the agent definition. A request cannot change either.

## Operation taxonomy

Operations are commands (mutate state and return a receipt), queries (read state), subscriptions (stream events), terminal-result waits, or background notifications. An operation a deployment does not serve is absent from its capability documents; a call to it anyway returns one typed unsupported result: on the A2A surface, `UnsupportedOperationError`, except that push notification configuration operations return `PushNotificationNotSupportedError` (`-32003`) when push is not declared (A2A 1.0.1 §3.3.4); on the management plane, the `NotImplemented` fault. `ExtendedAgentCardNotConfiguredError` (`-32007`) applies only to an agent that declares the extended card without configuring one, which v1 never does.

### A2A operations

| A2A operation | Kind | Bureau behavior | v1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `SendMessage` | Command | The returned task applies `configuration.historyLength`. A terminal-result wait is `SubscribeToTask` until the stream closes at a terminal state, or `GetTask` polling. There is no separate blocking wait operation.

The `fresh-from-artifact` conversation policy is not offered, because it needs a handoff artifact the Bureau's own resolver must vouch for |
| `bureau.goals.cancel` | Command | Cancels a goal and answers how far the cancellation has read back as finished. `expectedRevision` is compared against the goal's `revision` inside the store's compare-and-swap that records the cancellation (the revision advances on every write, a controller transition included): a mismatch is `StaleRevision` and nothing is written. Requires `bureau.goals:write` unless the caller is the owner |
| `bureau.memory.list`, `bureau.memory.search`, `bureau.memory.get` | Query | What the agent `agentId` remembers of the caller's chats with it (COR-1546): its memories, newest first, ranked against a `query`, and one with its lineage. A run writes its memory into the partition of the Bureau (the tenant) and the run's owner of record, under the collection named for its agent, so an agent remembers across every chat its owner has with it, and these operations read that partition through the governed memory the runs write into. A view is built field by field (`id`, `content`, `memoryClass`, `source`, `trust`, `createdAt`, and `sessionId`, the chat that produced it), and carries no governance envelope. `get` adds `lineage` from the ledger: `runId` (the run that wrote it, from its write event), `corrects`, and `correctedBy` (from the supersession event). Ranking has no score floor, so a search that finds nothing means the agent has no memory. Requires `bureau.memory:read`, which grants no one else's memory: a caller without it is `Forbidden` (see **Memory** under the predicate below). A Bureau composed without governed memory answers `UnsupportedCapability` |
| `bureau.memory.forget` | Command | Deletes one memory as a source deletion and answers the governed `receipt` and `deletion` as data. `deletion` is the status of the receipt row that removes the record: once it is `completed` the record never appears in a list, a search, a read, or a recall; `exempt` means a legal hold keeps it stored and listed, and the deletion resumes when the hold is released; `failed` means the step that removes it failed, and forgetting it again retries; `pending` means it is not settled. Bureau's maintenance pass (`maintain` and its automatic timer) runs `processDeletionPropagation()`, which settles the receipt's other targets. A host declares `not-retained` only for a target it keeps nothing in, and the text a run recalled is appended to the conversation and persisted with it, so scrubbing it is out of scope: the `durable-snapshots` target reports `pending` and then `failed` at its deadline. Another owner's memory and a missing one are `NotFound`. Requires `bureau.memory:write`; a caller without it is `Forbidden` |
| `bureau.memory.correct` | Command | Corrects one memory by superseding it with `content`, through governed memory's `forget({ mode: 'supersession' })`: COR-43's transition engine runs on the evidence projection storage and is not wired into governed memory. The successor is written by the caller as an `operator` source, in the original's memory class and the same collection; the original is kept and linked from the successor, and is no longer listed, searched, or recalled. A memory already corrected is `Conflict` (`data.reason` `memory-superseded`); a correction memory does not admit (its content policy, its quota) is `Unprocessable` (`memory-successor-not-admitted`), and the original stays in place. A blank `content` is `InvalidParams`; another owner's memory and a missing one are `NotFound`. Requires `bureau.memory:write`; a caller without it is `Forbidden` |
| `bureau.memory.share`, `bureau.memory.share.revoke` | Command | Lets the owner `granteeOwnerId` read one memory of the agent `agentId` (COR-1809), and withdraws that access. Both answer `{ id, changed, granteeOwnerId, grantedBy }`, where `grantedBy` is the caller's owner and governed memory's `memory.shared` or `memory.share.revoked` event records the same owner as its attribution and the grantee in its details; repeating a grant or a revoke is not an error (`changed: false`). | `bureau.memory.hold`, `bureau.memory.hold.release` | Command | Places and lifts a legal hold on one memory (COR-1809): while it is held neither `bureau.memory.forget` nor a retention sweep deletes it, and the deletion that waited on it resumes at release. | `bureau.memory.quarantine` | Command | Takes one memory, and everything derived from it, out of use pending inspection (COR-1809): it is no longer listed, searched, read, or recalled. | `bureau.memory.export` | Query | Exports every memory in the caller’s own private partition of the agent `agentId` that the caller may read, as `{ exportId, memories }` (COR-1809): `exportId` names the manifest governed memory stored (`null` for a caller that can own no memory), which a later deletion scrubs a record from, and a `memory.exported` ledger event records it under the caller's owner. The existing `bureau.events` and `operative.runs.events` operations are served through `createBureauHandler` over the generic Server-Sent Events route, as COR-1299 and COR-1300 require, with live-only semantics: a cursor resumes within one process lifetime and carries no promise across a restart. `bureau.tasks.events` is the durable, resumable equivalent. This answers COR-1298's third open question: both operations turn on `transports.http`, and their tests that assert WebSocket-only change accordingly.

`bureau.tasks.events` elements are `task.snapshot`, `task.status`, `task.resynced`, `task.gap`, and `task.removed`, each carrying `taskId`, the durable `cursor`, and `emittedAtMs`. A `task.status` carries the state, the finish reason, a status message of at most 1,000 characters, and, for a task that completed, the answer as one artifact of at most 64 KiB (`truncated` says when it was cut). A `task.resynced` element (COR-1669) restates where the task stands, with the same fields as a `task.status` and the `revision` of the task's record it described: when a durable append fails, the Bureau marks the task resync-pending in its record (`resyncPending`, beside a `revision` that counts each change of the record, both optional and read as absent in an older record), and then appends a durable `task.resynced` event under the task's run owner, in that owner's append order, so it describes the task after every earlier event; the mark clears only once the append has committed and only while the record is still at the revision it described. Every boot and every durable maintenance pass repairs each task that is marked, and each settled session run whose history still reaches back to its settlement (its oldest retained event is no later) and holds no event that says how it ended (a process that died after the settlement and before its append marks nothing). A client applies a `task.resynced` as it applies a `task.status`. A durable kind the projection does not list is dropped, and each durable event yields at most one element, so a cursor never names two. A task that ends on a `run.error` reports the finish reason that event carries: Bureau records `finishReason` on the `run.error` payload (COR-1627), the reason the run ends with (`maximum-steps` for a step limit, `error` for an unclassified failure), so a step-limit task's final status reads `maximum-steps` and not `null`. A `run.error` recorded before the payload carried it reads `null`. A review becoming pending is a durable `review.requested` event (the review identifier, its kind, and the signal name or call identifier, never the prompt or the arguments), which the stream shows as `input-required`; the event is an additive kind inside the existing record wrapper, so the history's `CURRENT_SCHEMA_VERSION` stays 1. A subscription that names no cursor begins with a `task.snapshot` and then replays what the history retains, and a stream ends at the task's terminal status once no review is open, or at its removal. A schedule fire is not a session run, so it records no `run.*` events; its end is a durable `schedule.fire.settled` event (the run identifier and the final state, recorded once under the fire's own run owner after its settlement is written, so it is an additive kind inside the existing record wrapper and the history's `CURRENT_SCHEMA_VERSION` stays 1), which every Bureau sharing the stores follows, so a stream ends on a Bureau that did not settle the fire. It is the fire's run, not its schedule, that carries the event, because a schedule's durable page never carries a fire and a fire's task record names no schedule. The event is appended once the settlement is written, by every route that writes one, with a retry, and a settled fire within the event retention window that lacks the event is announced at the next boot of any Bureau on the store, so a stream open on a peer across a Bureau that died between the two ends then, dated where the fire ended. A fire settled before the retention cutoff is not announced again, because retention removes an event with its deduplication marker and would otherwise see one fire's end recorded again at every cycle; a client that reconnects reads the settled record at once. The stream also reacts when it can no longer trust the history it follows. A gap in that history (retention overtook it, possibly retiring the fire's own event) and the subscription ending on a storage fault each make the stream read the settled record again: a fire that is over ends the stream with its terminal status, and any other fire ends it with the control plane's `Unavailable` fault (reason `history-unavailable`, a message telling the client to reconnect), so the client reconnects, reads the record at once, and a new subscription follows the history from there. The stream does not retry the subscription itself. The cutoff is each Bureau's own `retention.events` and clock, so Bureaus that share a store with different retention, or with clocks apart, can announce a fire in the band between their cutoffs once more. Only runs in a context record durable `run.*` events, which every task does when the Bureau keeps a durable event history. A Bureau with no persistent storage keeps no durable history, and `bureau.tasks.events` answers it with `UnsupportedCapability`.

A caller that can only write is told the receipt and nothing more: `bureau.tasks.cancel` carries the task view (owner, context, agent, status, open waits) only for a caller that could read the task, and `bureau.contexts.delete` names the tasks it cancelled only to the context's owner or a caller holding both `bureau.contexts:read` and `bureau.tasks:read`. Both operations authorize against the exact session or task they act on: `bureau.contexts.get` and `delete` load the session once, judge its own recorded owner, and delete only that incarnation (`Bureau.contexts.delete(id, { incarnation })`, compare-and-swapped in the session store), so a context id deleted and created again for another owner in between is neither read nor deleted. A completed task's answer rides on its `task.status` as the `artifact`.

A crash can leave a task record behind its run, so boot recovery reconciles: a task record with no settlement whose durable run is terminal is settled from the durable state, and a task that retention removed stays removed. A recovered run settles its task through one internal hook (`catalog-run-settlement.ts`) that fires when the run ends, dispatched by this process or recovered after a restart, and publishes only an end the durable run actually reached: a handle that rejects because the engine was disposed (a graceful shutdown) publishes nothing, so the task stays open and cancelable and the run's snapshot reference stays unsettled until the run resumes at the next boot. The hook also publishes recovered child runs and recovered goal attempts, which are not tasks, so each subscriber filters what it needs: the task settler skips a run the child topology holds and a goal attempt, and the snapshot-reference subscriber settles every recovered run's reference; a settled task whose review is still pending is not removed by retention until the review is resolved or canceled; the task history of a context is erased whether the context is deleted or expires through `sessionStore.cleanup`; a deletion that fails retryably after its live runs were aborted leaves the context in place with cancelled tasks, and a retry deletes it. Settlement and cleanup writes are awaited by `shutdown()` before storage closes, with the wait for the settlements being published and for cleanup acknowledgements each bounded by event-loop turns (one that arrives later is not recorded), and every canceled terminal, a shutdown's abort included, follows its run's cleanup.

`bureau.tasks.cancel` and `bureau.contexts.delete` take `waitMilliseconds` (default 0, at most 60,000), the longest they wait for a cleanup acknowledgement still outstanding; with the default the receipt is whatever has settled, and `bureau.tasks.get` carries the same cleanup state afterwards. `Bureau.contexts.delete` resolves a report of the tasks that were live in the session, each with its run's cleanup acknowledgement, so the operation reports without a second deletion method.

## Extensions

Bureau publishes these A2A extensions. Each URI names a specification document, written in COR-1474 and kept at `packages/weft/documentation/a2a/extensions/`; `packages/weft/scripts/check-a2a-extension-schemas.ts` compiles the schemas in them and validates their examples. The URIs are stable wire identifiers that clients compare, not links a client must resolve (decision 2026-10-22). The documents specify the extensions; the implementation is COR-903.

| Extension | URI | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Durable history | `…/a2a/extensions/durable-history-v1.md` | On A2A `SubscribeToTask`: resumption through the standard `Last-Event-ID` request header (the request carries only `tenant` and `id`); the first event is always the current `Task`, whose extension metadata carries its cursor C, the requested cursor, and, when the requested cursor is below the retention floor, the gap; then only durable events after C, each with its cursor as the SSE `id`. Ephemeral frames carry no `id`. A2A never replays events older than the `Task` it just sent; full event replay is `bureau.tasks.events` on the management plane |
| Command receipts | `…/a2a/extensions/command-receipts-v1.md` | The receipt state, `expectedRevision`, and `deadline` on `SendMessage` and `SendStreamingMessage`, the two A2A commands whose request carries a message, and a typed conflict on reuse. `CancelTask` and push-configuration `Delete` have fixed shapes with no carrier and rely on their specified idempotency instead. A2A does not make `Create` idempotent, so Bureau does: a `Create` naming an existing configuration `id` for the same task and caller with the same webhook URL, `token`, and authentication, or omitting `id` with the same three as an existing configuration, returns that configuration rather than registering a second one; a deleted configuration never matches, and a `Create` naming a deleted configuration's `id` is rejected with `-32602`, and a `Create` reusing an existing `id` with any of the three changed is rejected with `-32602` (COR-1470). A new configuration's projector checkpoint is set, in the same write as the configuration, to the cursor C read under the task's mutation barrier, so every update after C reaches it and none before |
| Cleanup | `…/a2a/extensions/cleanup-v1.md` | The `CleanupAcknowledgement` and review detail on task status, so a canceled task can report whether cleanup finished |

`…` is `https://github.com/stevekinney/weft/blob/main/packages/weft/documentation`.

Bureau also advertises one third-party extension, A2UI's A2A extension at the URI the pinned A2UI release (`v0.9.1`) defines, as optional. It carries interactive surfaces to A2A clients that render them (experimental, pinned to A2UI v0.9.1); its card `params` are `supportedCatalogIds` (the basic catalog and Cinder's), `acceptsInlineCatalogs: false`, and `experimental: true`. Bureau's own rules below apply to it as well. A2A reads (`GetTask`, `ListTasks`, `SubscribeToTask`) declare no client catalogs and so offer the basic catalog only; a Cinder surface reads as its text summary there. A2A streams carry surface updates for a client that activated the extension (COR-837): `SendStreamingMessage` and `SubscribeToTask` send each surface revision once as a `statusUpdate` of the current state, with a `deleteSurface` part for a surface torn down and the final frozen revision ahead of the terminal status (when the task record already shows the end), under the cursor of the history event that changed it ([interactive surfaces](./interactive-surface.md#over-a2a-experimental)); `SubscribeToTask` declares no catalogs, so it streams basic-catalog surfaces as data and others as text. A client that did not activate the extension gets no per-revision surface frames, and the `input-required` status no longer duplicates surfaces. The AG-UI mount carries the same surfaces as `ACTIVITY_SNAPSHOT`s for a client that opts in (see [interactive surfaces](./interactive-surface.md#a2ui-over-ag-ui)).

The rules for every extension:

- **Activation.** A client activates an extension per request with the `A2A-Extensions` header, or the binding's equivalent. The response echoes the extensions Bureau applied. The one exception is A2UI's extension, which Bureau also treats as activated through either of the mechanisms the pinned A2UI v0.9.1 specification defines: the `X-A2A-Extensions` header, accepted as an A2UI-only alias, or `metadata.a2uiClientCapabilities` on a message. The cross-origin CORS allowlist allows and exposes `X-A2A-Extensions` for that reason.
- **Versioning.** A breaking change publishes a new URI with the next major version. Bureau never falls back to an older version on its own.
- **Optional by declaration.** Every card entry has `required: false`.
- **Fail-closed as a client.** When an operative calls a peer whose card marks an extension `required` and Bureau does not implement it, Bureau refuses the call with `ExtensionSupportRequiredError` rather than sending a request the peer will misread.
- **Typed substate.** Extension data lives in the extension's own namespace inside `metadata`, with its own schema; it never repurposes a core field.

## Receipt state machine

A command's receipt is a projection of the store that admitted it: the COR-424 ledger for run creation, Weft's mailbox for commands to an existing resource, and Weft's outbox for deliveries. The contract's receipt states, and which stores produce them:

| Receipt state | Meaning | Produced by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `accepted` | Durably admitted; nothing applied yet | Ledger pending entry; mailbox `accepted`, `available` |
| `rejected` | Refused at admission; nothing will be applied | Validation, authorization, mailbox `rejected` (backlog full) |
| `pending` | Claimed and in progress | Mailbox `claimed`; ledger in-flight entry |
| `applied` | The mutation took effect | Ledger complete; mailbox `applied` |
| `observed` | Applied, and the effect is visible in durable history | Durable history append for the resource |
| `superseded` | Replaced by a later command before it applied | Review `superseded`; session input `superseded` |
| `failed` | Accepted but could not apply | Mailbox `dead-lettered` or failure reasons |
| `cancel-requested` | Cancellation recorded; the work has not stopped | Mailbox `cancellation-requested`; run `aborting` |
| `canceled` | Stopped before or during application | Mailbox `cancelled`; run `aborted` |
| `cleanup-pending` | Terminal, with cleanup still running | `CleanupAcknowledgement` not yet settled |
| `cleaned-up` | Terminal, with cleanup `completed` or `not-required` | `CleanupAcknowledgement` |
| `expired` | The command's deadline passed before it applied | Mailbox `deadline-exceeded`; ledger deadline |
| `cleanup-failed` | Terminal, and cleanup definitely failed | `CleanupAcknowledgement` `failed` |
| `cleanup-unresolved` | Terminal, and cleanup could not be confirmed; it may still be running, so an external effect may still occur and a client must not treat the work as safely retryable | `CleanupAcknowledgement` `unresolved` with `timed-out`, `unreachable`, or `persistence-failed` |
| `unknown-outcome` | An external effect may or may not have happened | Outbox `unknown-outcome`; `CleanupAcknowledgement` `unresolved` with `unknown-effect` |

Which states each operation can produce:

| Operation | States |
| --- | --- |
| `SendMessage` or `SendStreamingMessage` creating a task | `accepted`, `rejected`, `pending`, `applied`, `observed`, `expired` |
| `SendMessage` or `SendStreamingMessage` continuing a task | `accepted`, `rejected`, `pending`, `applied`, `observed`, `superseded`, `failed`, `expired` |
| `bureau.tasks.cancel` (A2A `CancelTask` returns the task itself, which carries no receipt) | `cancel-requested`, `canceled`, `cleanup-pending`, `cleaned-up`, `cleanup-failed`, `cleanup-unresolved`, `unknown-outcome`, `rejected` |
| `bureau.contexts.delete` | `applied`, `rejected`, `cleanup-pending`, `cleaned-up`, `cleanup-failed`, `cleanup-unresolved`, `unknown-outcome` |
| `bureau.contexts.input` | `admitted`, `replayed`, with a receipt state of `accepted`, `promoted` or `canceled` |
| `bureau.contexts.steer` | `accepted`, `replayed`, `conflict`, `rejected` (`model-not-allowed`, `stale-revision`, `shutting-down`, `policy-denied`), `unsupported-capability` |
| `bureau.contexts.fork` | the new `contextId`, with `carriedOverride` and `carriedEffort`: the steering outcome above, or `null` |
| `bureau.contexts.rename` | `{ contextId, title }`: the title now stored, `null` once removed |
| `bureau.reviews.resolve` | `applied`, `rejected`, `superseded` |
| Management delivery commands (`bureau.deliveries.retry`, `bureau.deliveries.cancel`; A2A push-configuration methods return their fixed objects and carry no receipt) | `accepted`, `pending`, `applied`, `failed`, `canceled`, `unknown-outcome` |
| Schedule and goal commands | `applied`, `rejected`, `cancel-requested`, `canceled`, `cleanup-pending`, `cleaned-up`, `cleanup-failed`, `cleanup-unresolved` |

The management receipts map onto observed facts. `bureau.tasks.cancel` answers `cancel-requested` while the run has not stopped, and then the state its `CleanupAcknowledgement` ends in: `cleanup-pending` while it is outstanding, `cleaned-up` for `completed` or `not-required`, `cleanup-failed` for `failed`, `cleanup-unresolved` for `unresolved` with `timed-out`, `unreachable`, or `persistence-failed`, and `unknown-outcome` for `unresolved` with `unknown-effect`. `canceled` is the receipt for a durable run that this process holds no live handle for and whose workflow cancellation was requested: it claims the stop and nothing about cleanup, because no acknowledgement exists to report. `rejected` is a cancel of a task that finished without being canceled (reason `already-terminal`): nothing is applied, and it is a receipt, not a `Conflict` fault; a repeated cancel of a task already canceled answers its current cleanup receipt. `bureau.contexts.delete` answers `applied` when no task was live, and `rejected` (reason `shutting-down`) once `shutdown()` has closed admission. Otherwise it answers the aggregate of its live tasks' cleanup states, the least settled winning in this order: `cleanup-pending` (a request still outstanding counts here), `cleanup-unresolved` (a stop with no acknowledgement behind it counts here), `unknown-outcome`, `cleanup-failed`, `cleaned-up`; the response lists each task's own state.

A `bureau.reviews.resolve` whose decision is `reject` and that lands is `applied`: the response's `decision` carries `reject`, and `rejected` stays the receipt of a command refused at admission, which v1 reports as a fault. The optional `round` input fences a decision on the round the caller read: a re-gated approval keeps its id and advances its round, and a `round` the review has moved past is a `Conflict` whose `reason` begins `REVIEW_ROUND_CHANGED: `, with no receipt, no decision, no audit entry, and nothing written; a human wait is always at round 0, and a call naming no `round` is unfenced. An AG-UI resume is fenced the same way: a tool approval's entry carries the round its interrupt showed as `metadata['@lostgradient/bureau'].round`, and one the approval has moved past is refused with the `RUN_ERROR` code `REVIEW_ROUND_CHANGED` before anything is decided (COR-1714).

## Retry, concurrency, and ordering

- **Exact retry.** The same principal, agent, A2A operation version, `messageId` (and, for a continuation, target task), and payload digest returns the original receipt and task. Exactly one run exists.
- **Conflicting reuse.** The same principal, agent, A2A operation version, and `messageId` (and, for a continuation, target task) with a different payload digest returns, to a client that activated command receipts, a typed conflict carrying the original receipt, and to any other client a standard JSON-RPC `-32602` error stating that the `messageId` was reused with a different payload, without receipt metadata; either way it is recorded as its own failed ledger entry (COR-424's `BureauLedgerConflictError`, reason `payload-mismatch`). The original command is untouched.
- **Ordering.** Messages to one task are applied in admission order through that task's mailbox. Different tasks are independent; there is no ordering across them. Because Weft's mailbox binds identifiers per task, a continuation's idempotency scope also includes its target task: the same `messageId` sent as continuations to two different tasks is two commands, while an exact retry to the same task returns the original receipt.
- **Concurrency.** State transitions are compare-and-swap on the record. Two concurrent commands to one resource produce one winner; the other observes the new revision.
- **Stale target.** A command carrying `expectedRevision` that no longer matches returns the stale-revision fault and changes nothing. On A2A, two concurrent sends naming one `expectedRevision` have one winner, whether they reach one Bureau instance or several sharing a store (COR-1694): before its claim and before any effect, a send reserves its message's slot in the task's A2A history with a compare-and-set on the history's length, the revision's durable part, and the send that loses it is refused as stale. A send whose effect is then refused gives its slot back. A slot reserved by a send that died before claiming stays until that send's exact retry finishes it; a claim left pending already holds its slot, so its retry finishes the effect it reserved. Giving a slot back shortens the history again, so a client that read the revision while the slot was held can name a revision that recurs.
- **Duplicate delivery.** A command delivered twice, for example by a retrying proxy, is an exact retry.
- **Expiry.** A run-creation binding is held for as long as its task is non-terminal, and its retention window starts only when the task settles, so a delayed retry of a long-running task never admits a second run. After the idempotency binding's retention, 24 hours by default, the mailbox deletes the command record and its binding (`packages/weft/src/core/mailbox-maintenance.ts`), so a query for the old receipt returns not-found and the same `messageId` admits a new command. Clients that retry later than that must mint a new identifier. A command whose deadline passes before it applies is `expired`.
- **Disconnect.** Closing a stream or connection changes nothing about the task. The client reattaches with `SubscribeToTask`.
- **Process restart.** With persistent storage, ledger entries, mailbox receipts, outbox deliveries, and durable history survive a restart. `BureauOptions.persistence` is optional and an ephemeral Bureau has no durable history, so `createBureauHandler` derives each extension from the concrete stores Bureau actually composed, never from `BureauOptions.persistence` alone: command receipts only when the persistent COR-424 ledger and the session-input mailbox exist, durable history only when the durable engine and its event history exist, and push only when both the outbox and the durable event history exist, because push notifications are projections of durable history. A deployment missing a store omits the matching extension from every card and makes no guarantee that depends on it. COR-424's fault points define run creation's behavior at each crash boundary: a retry after a crash never starts a second run once a ledger entry exists.
- **No exactly-once external effects.** Bureau guarantees one model run per command identity. It does not guarantee that a tool's or a webhook receiver's side effect happened exactly once; a crash after an external effect and before settlement reports `unknown-outcome`.

## Query and event model

One model underlies every surface. Resource snapshots come from the resource's own store; events come from Bureau's durable event history. The A2A stream, the AG-UI stream, `bureau.tasks.events`, and push notifications are projections of that history, and each may expose a strict subset without changing what an event means. Push delivery is projected from the history by a projector that persists, per push configuration, the last cursor it enqueued, so a crash between a history append and its outbox enqueue is repaired on restart by replaying from that checkpoint (COR-903). A checkpoint that retention has overtaken cannot replay, so the projector takes the task's current `Task` snapshot and its cursor C together under the snapshot fence, enqueues one delivery carrying that snapshot, and advances the checkpoint to C, so no event older than the snapshot is sent after it. If a durable append fails, Bureau first marks the task resync-pending in its persisted A2A record, then repairs the history itself by appending, under the task's mutation barrier so no concurrent mutation can interleave, a `task.resynced` durable event carrying the task's current projection and revision, clearing the mark only once that append commits, so a later successful append cannot hide the missed update. Every projection of history serves it: push delivers it as a current `Task` snapshot, A2A streams as a status update plus one artifact update per artifact, and `bureau.tasks.events` as a resync event, so a disconnected management consumer that resumes from `Last-Event-ID` still receives the repair. Because a crash can interrupt that, Bureau also, on every start and on every durable maintenance pass, appends `task.resynced` for each task that is marked resync-pending or whose record revision is newer than the revision of its last durable event, so no window between a mutation and its projections loses an update and a repaired task is not resent. The projector keeps at most one unsettled delivery per configuration, enqueuing the next only after the previous one is terminal, so a webhook receives a configuration's updates in generation order (A2A 1.0.1 §3.5.2) even though Weft's outbox lets a due delivery pass one in retry backoff.

| Element | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Durable cursor | An opaque string. Clients compare cursors for equality only; today's decimal encoding is not part of the contract. |
| Retention floor | A deployment-configured history retention, `BureauOptions.retention.events` (default 7 days, `'forever'` to disable). Each durable maintenance pass calls the fleet feed's `retain()` for events older than the window, so the floor advances over a contiguous prefix and never past an event newer than the cutoff. `BureauOptions.retention.tasks` (default 30 days) bounds tasks the same way: maintenance deletes a terminal task older than the window through the run store, so `run.removed` fires, and only once its run-creation binding is gone (one unreadable binding record makes the pass fail closed until repaired). A task counts as old from its closing `run.completed` or `run.aborted` action. Only runs that settled in the process's lifetime are candidates: a terminal run from before a restart is not reattached and never receives `run.removed` from this pass. |
| Gap response | On A2A, a resume below the floor is reported in the initial `Task`'s durable-history metadata (requested cursor and first retained cursor), keeping the `Task` first as `SubscribeToTask` requires. On `bureau.tasks.events`, it yields one gap event carrying both cursors, then the events from the floor. `subscribeEventHistory`'s silent gap skipping is replaced, not projected: its listener receives one `history.gap` event whose payload carries `requestedCursor` and `firstRetainedCursor`, and its cursor is the last one before the floor. A subscription whose undelivered backlog the floor overtakes while it is open receives the same event before any later one (its requested cursor is the cursor of the last event it was sent, a previous gap event included); a subscription drained to the tail is never sent one. `firstRetainedCursor` is the canonical shape on the subscriber-facing event; the page API's `firstRetainedSequence` is pre-existing. The `run.removed` event that records a deletion ages out under `retention.events` like any other. |
| Snapshot plus tail | On A2A, every subscription, fresh or resumed, receives the current `Task` at cursor C first, then only durable events after C, so the client's state never moves backwards; the `Task` already contains every status and artifact a resumed client missed. On `bureau.tasks.events`, a resume with `Last-Event-ID` above the floor replays every durable event after that cursor in order, with monotonic ids, before live events, and a fresh subscription receives the snapshot first. The initial `Task` and its cursor C are read together: Bureau holds this task's mutation barrier, so no new mutation of the task commits while the pair is read, waits for the task's in-flight durable appends (which the durable-event producer already tracks) to settle, then reads the fleet-wide tail C, builds the `Task` from the task record, and accepts the pair only if the durable history holds no event for this task after the record's last folded cursor and at or before C. Otherwise, because the mutation barrier admits no new change, each such event is one the task record already reflects but whose fold was not persisted (a crash between the append and the cursor update), so Bureau verifies it against the record, advances and persists the folded cursor past it, and accepts the pair; the read never loops on unchanged state. An idle task therefore succeeds on the first read even while other tasks append, so no event between the snapshot and the tail is lost or duplicated. |
| Ephemeral frames | Step, delta, and tool frames from the in-memory run feed reach AG-UI streams live, and never A2A streams. They carry no resumable cursor and are not replayed; reconnecting yields the snapshot and the durable tail. |
| Slow consumers | Durable-history subscriptions are pull-driven and never buffer unboundedly. On management streams, ephemeral frames beyond a stream's buffer are dropped and the stream emits one frames-dropped marker (`frames.dropped`, positioned at the last event the reader received, so its `sequence` and `cursor` repeat that event's and are not unique across kinds: never use them as an SSE id with a skip-if-not-greater rule; the stream then resumes after that cursor, replaying whatever the in-memory log still retains, which is the oldest retained event when the log has evicted past the reader's cursor). On an A2A stream, which must deliver the same events in the same order as every other active stream for the task (A2A 1.0.1 §3.5.2), a subscriber that falls behind its buffer is closed instead, and reconnects with `Last-Event-ID`. An A2A tail whose next cursor has fallen below the retention floor (a `fleet:gap`) is closed the same way rather than silently skipping events, so the reconnect's initial `Task` supplies the current state. An AG-UI stream whose reader falls behind its buffer is closed with `RUN_ERROR` `STREAM_OVERFLOW` rather than thinned, because a dropped text delta would silently corrupt a transcript the client rebuilds from deltas; the client attaches again and the snapshot the new stream opens with restores what it missed. |
| A2A projection of live frames | A2A streams carry only the protocol's `StreamResponse` kinds: the initial `Task`, `TaskStatusUpdateEvent`, and `TaskArtifactUpdateEvent`, and every active stream for a task receives the same sequence (A2A 1.0.1 §3.5.2). Step, delta, and tool frames are therefore never sent on A2A; they reach clients only through AG-UI. The frames-dropped marker is sent on management streams only. Status and artifact changes reach A2A as durable events. The durable-event producer appends a task's events strictly in task-revision order, starting an append only after the previous one for that task commits, so the fleet cursor order matches each task's generation order even when an append is slow. Where a durable history is composed and delivering, the A2A projection suppresses the live copy of every durable event kind (`RUN_DURABLE_EVENT_TYPES`, such as run completion, error, abort, and tripwire), so each transition arrives exactly once with its cursor. The live copy is suppressed only once its append commits; if the append fails, the retained live copy is projected without a cursor and the stream closes after it, so the reconnect's initial `Task` supplies the state. Where none is composed, the live fallback subscribes before it reads its initial `Task`, under the same mutation barrier the durable fence uses, so no status or artifact change falls between them; every live status and artifact change, interrupted states such as `INPUT_REQUIRED` and `AUTH_REQUIRED` included, is projected from the live source without a cursor, so both compositions produce the same sequence and differ only in whether events carry cursors. |
| Audit | A separate, allowlisted, append-only compliance record (`packages/bureau/src/audit-trail.ts`). Events are not audit records, and the audit trail is not an event stream. |
| Telemetry | Traces and metrics follow `traceparent` and are not part of the contract. |

## Authorization

The tenant is the deployment, and authorization inside it is owner of record plus operator scopes.

- **Owner of record.** Every resource records its owning principal when it is created, and the record survives restart. The owning principal is a credential-specific identity: a JWT `sub`, an API key's subject from `resolveApiKeyPrincipal`, or an mTLS client's stable service identity, returned by the host-supplied `MTLSConfig.resolvePrincipal` from the verified certificate that the host-supplied `MTLSConfig.getPeerCertificate(request)` provides (both added to Weft by COR-1479, since `AuthContext` carries no certificate today and a fetch handler cannot see the TLS session), so that a renewed or rotated certificate keeps the same owner and no spoofable request data names the owner; the certificate fingerprint is recorded only for audit and revocation. Weft's shared fallbacks (`api-key-caller` for every static key, `mtls-caller` for unforwarded mTLS) and JWTs without `sub` are rejected for owner-scoped operations, because different callers would compare as one owner; several keys may share an owner only through an explicit alias in `resolveApiKeyPrincipal`. Work started by the scheduler, recovery, or expiry carries a named service principal such as `system:scheduler`, never an absent owner. The persisted owner string is namespaced by authentication method so identities from different credential types never collide: `jwt:<issuer>:<sub>`, `api-key:<subject>`, and `mtls:<service identity>`, where every component is percent-encoded (`encodeURIComponent`, which encodes `:`) so no two distinct tuples produce the same string, and the JWT issuer is the validated issuer from OIDC discovery (COR-1479) or, without discovery, the configured `issuer` the verifier checks against `iss`; `createBureauHandler` rejects at construction a JWT configuration with neither, so the issuer is never an unvalidated claim or empty; the `system:` namespace is reserved for Bureau's own service principals and rejected from any credential; sharing across namespaces happens only through an explicit alias. Weft today forbids `resolveApiKeyPrincipal` alongside `jwt` (`validateAuthConfig`), and static keys share one subject, so a deployment that offers both JWT and API keys cannot yet give API-key callers an owner; until COR-1479 lets the resolver handle `X-API-Key` while Bearer tokens fall through to JWT verification, `createBureauHandler` rejects that combination at construction and the card never advertises a scheme whose callers cannot own resources. Static `AuthConfig.apiKeys` are rejected at construction whatever else is configured, because every static key authenticates as the shared `api-key-caller`; API-key authentication requires `resolveApiKeyPrincipal`, and A2A 1.0.1 §12.6 requires every advertised scheme to work.
- **Owners see their own.** A principal can list, read, command, observe, and cancel resources it owns. On the A2A surface every lookup and listing is also scoped to the opted-in agent of the mount the request arrived on, so one agent's endpoint never reveals or acts on another agent's tasks.
- **Operator scopes.** Bureau registers its own scopes (`bureau.tasks:read`, `bureau.tasks:write`, `bureau.reviews:read`, `bureau.reviews:resolve`, `bureau.deliveries:read`, `bureau.deliveries:write`, `bureau.audit:read`, `bureau.schedules:read`, `bureau.schedules:write`, `bureau.goals:read`, `bureau.goals:write`, `bureau.contexts:read`, `bureau.contexts:write`, `bureau.agents:read`, `bureau.agents:write`, `bureau.workflows:read`, `bureau.workflows:write`, `bureau.assets:read`, `bureau.assets:write`, `bureau.memory:read`, `bureau.memory:write`, `bureau.memory:govern`, `bureau.decisions:write`, `bureau.settings:read`, `bureau.settings:write`, `bureau.diagnostics:read`, `bureau.deployments:read`, `bureau.deployments:write`, and `bureau.events:observe-all`). A write scope does not imply its read scope; operators hold both beside Weft's closed scope list. They grant cross-owner access only to management operations, never to the A2A surface, where A2A's `ListTasks` stays caller-scoped. The two `bureau.assets` scopes are the exception to that convention: they gate who may upload and read assets and grant nothing across owners, because `AssetService` authorizes by an asset's owner and readers alone. The three `bureau.memory` scopes are the same kind of exception: they grant nothing across owners, and a memory operation needs its scope even for the caller's own memory (see **Memory** under the predicate below). `createBureauHandler` registers all of them and grants all of them to the local caller, `anonymous:local`, under `unauthenticatedAccess: 'allow'`.
- **Not-found-shaped denial.** A resource the caller may not see answers exactly as one that does not exist, on every surface.
- **Identifiers are not credentials.** Knowing a task, context, review, or asset identifier grants nothing.
- **Wildcard observation** of every present and future task is the separate `bureau.events:observe-all` scope; a read scope does not imply it.
- **The only public paths** are each opted-in agent's Agent Card and, when the card is signed, the JWKS its signature names. Every other path authenticates (COR-718's "no unauthenticated paths" criterion is amended to say so).
- **Public discovery under mTLS.** When a deployment authenticates with mutual TLS, a client without a certificate is rejected during the TLS handshake, before any public route runs. Such a deployment serves the Agent Card and JWKS from a listener that does not request client certificates, at the separate origin it configures as `discoveryUrl`, and every other route stays on the mTLS listener at `publicUrl`; `createBureauHandler` exposes its discovery routes as a separately mountable handler for that purpose. Weft's `createAuthenticator` today treats an otherwise uncredentialed request as authenticated mTLS (`packages/weft/src/server/authentication/index.ts`), which is an authentication bypass whenever client certificates are optional. A separate Weft fix makes it report `mtls` only with a verified peer certificate, and no deployment may make client certificates optional until that fix lands.
- **A2A's `tenant` field** carries the deployment identifier, the tenant; the interface URL, which is the mount, selects the opted-in agent. A request whose `tenant` differs from the mounted interface's `AgentInterface.tenant` is rejected with `-32602` before the SDK handler runs, so no tenant-scoped store is addressed by a caller-chosen partition. It is not a security boundary.

| --- | --- | --- |

| Memory | An agent's memory is served only to the owner whose chats wrote it, and only with the operation's scope: `bureau.memory:read` for `bureau.memory.list`, `search`, `get`, and `export`, `bureau.memory:write` for `forget`, `correct`, `share`, `share.revoke`, and `quarantine`, and `bureau.memory:govern` for `hold` and `hold.release`. This is a deliberate departure from the owner-sees-own rule above, because what an agent remembers is not something a credential reaches by default. Another owner's memory, and a missing one, is `NotFound`. ## Error contract

Errors are versioned with the surface that returns them, machine-readable, and, when produced after the request is parsed, carry the request's correlation identifier and, for commands, the command identifier; authentication (401) and rate-limit (429) rejections happen before parsing and carry no correlation identifier. Weft's `FaultCode` gains the categories marked new.

| Category | A2A surface | Weft fault | HTTP (management plane) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Validation | JSON-RPC `-32602` | `InvalidParams` | 400 |
| Unsupported content | `ContentTypeNotSupportedError` (`-32005`) when a message part's media type is outside the agent's `defaultInputModes` or skill input modes | `Unprocessable` | 415 |
| Authentication | HTTP 401 | `Unauthorized` | 401 |
| Authorization | Not found (denial is not-found-shaped) | `NotFound` | 404 |
| Not found | `TaskNotFoundError` (`-32001`) | `NotFound` | 404 |
| Conflict | Command-receipts extension conflict: an A2A error whose `ErrorInfo` reason is `CONFLICT` | `Conflict` | 409 |
| Stale revision | Command-receipts extension stale revision: an A2A error whose `ErrorInfo` reason is `STALE_REVISION` | `StaleRevision` (new) | 409 |
| Unsupported capability | `UnsupportedOperationError` (`-32004`), `ExtensionSupportRequiredError` (`-32008`), `VersionNotSupportedError` (`-32009`) | `NotImplemented`, `UnsupportedCapability` (new) | 400, 501 |
| Rate limit | HTTP 429 | `RateLimited` (new) | 429 |
| Deadline | A2A has no deadline code. A creation command that expires before applying projects the new task as `TASK_STATE_FAILED` with a deadline reason; an expired continuation leaves the existing task in its current, still-interrupted state and reports the command's `expired` receipt; an expired request is JSON-RPC `-32603` with `Timeout` data | `Timeout` | 504 |
| Cancellation | `TaskNotCancelableError` (`-32002`) for a task that is `COMPLETED`, `FAILED`, or `REJECTED`; `CANCELED` (A2A 1.0.1 §3.1.5 names an already-canceled task as not cancelable, so a repeated cancel has the same effect and the same error), and `TaskNotFoundError` once the task is purged (§3.3.1) | `Conflict` (Weft keeps no cancellation category), for the A2A surface only: `bureau.tasks.cancel` reports a task that finished without being canceled as the `rejected` receipt, and a repeated cancel as its current cleanup receipt, so it raises no fault for either | 409 |
| Unavailable dependency | JSON-RPC `-32603` with retry detail | `Unavailable` (new) | 503 |
| Internal failure | JSON-RPC `-32603` | `EngineFailure` | 500 |

The HTTP column applies to the management plane. On the A2A JSON-RPC mounts, every application-level error is a JSON-RPC error envelope with HTTP 200, as the pinned SDK returns it; only authentication (401) and rate limiting (429), which are rejected before the JSON-RPC handler runs, use HTTP statuses there. `ErrorInfo` reasons are UPPER_SNAKE_CASE identifiers (`CONFLICT`, `STALE_REVISION`, `TIMEOUT`, `RATE_LIMITED`, `UNAVAILABLE`), as `google.rpc.ErrorInfo` requires, not Weft's fault names.

A2A 1.0.1 §9.5 reserves the whole range `-32001` to `-32099` for A2A errors, and Weft's own codes sit inside it (`-32010` to `-32099`). The two surfaces therefore never share an endpoint: the A2A mount answers only with A2A error codes, and its `error.data` is the array §9.5 requires, each entry carrying `@type`: for a core A2A error, the `google.rpc.ErrorInfo` §9.5 defines, with domain `a2a-protocol.org` and its standardized reason (such as `TASK_NOT_FOUND`); for a fault with no A2A code, an `ErrorInfo` with domain `bureau` and the UPPER_SNAKE_CASE reason listed above, never the Weft fault name. Weft fault objects, with their `weftCode` and `httpStatus`, appear only on the management plane's endpoint.

## Untrusted input and catalog identity

Per-run input from outside Bureau carries only the message, its parts, and the envelope fields above. An inbound message whose `role` is not `ROLE_USER` is rejected with `-32602`, so a caller cannot forge an agent-authored history entry. AB-15's hardening removes everything else from untrusted input:

- `systemPrompt`, `maximumSteps`, and `maximumTokens`, which `bureau.tasks.start` does not accept either: they belong to the agent's own definition.
- `principal` in any payload. The principal is the authenticated caller.
- Provider and route overrides, except through steering policy profiles the agent's definition allows. A model override is not accepted from a payload either, except as the exact `{ provider, model }` `bureau.contexts.steer` takes, which Bureau checks against the models the agent itself allows (`bureau.agents.models`) when it is admitted. An effort override is accepted from a payload only as the `effort` key of `bureau.contexts.steer`, which Bureau checks against the efforts its model catalog holds (COR-1935).
- Tool lists, tool approvals, and grants.

AB-22's immutable catalog is the only source of external identity and capability metadata. A card's identity is the catalog's agent name and `definitionRevision`; its descriptive fields come from the agent definition (COR-1301); its visibility comes from the A2A opt-in (`a2aAgents`, or the file's `a2a`). Nothing in a request can change any of them.

## Agent coordination

Bureau manages many operatives, and they coordinate in two ways.

- **Parent and child.** A parent dispatches, waits on, signals, and cancels its children through the typed in-process child API, with `cascade` or `detach` cancellation, `closed()` cleanup, and delegation grants. This is unchanged; children are internal resources that appear on the parent task's status detail.
- **Peers.** An operative communicates with another agent by Agent Card over A2A, whether the peer runs in this Bureau or another. The calling operative is an A2A client (COR-905), and operatives are that issue's named consumer. A call to a peer in the same Bureau goes through the same A2A path, so a peer can move between deployments without the caller changing.

Peers are a trust boundary. A message or artifact from a peer is untrusted input whose provenance is the peer's Agent Card identity, and it enters memory only as untrusted evidence with that provenance. Nothing from memory, transcripts, or other tasks reaches a peer unless the operative puts it in the outgoing message.

This supersedes COR-807, which rejected direct peer messaging for lack of a consumer. The [runtime contracts](../../../documentation/runtime-contracts.md#parent-child-and-peer-communication) peer paragraph describes what shipped with COR-905.

As built, the client is `createA2APeerClient` in `@lostgradient/weft/a2a`, and Operative's `createPeerCallTool` is the typed surface an operative calls it through.

- **Card.** The card is read from `/.well-known/agent-card.json`, cached per client for five minutes (`cardCacheMs`), and read again after an `unauthorized`, `version-not-supported`, `extension-support-required` or `content-type-not-supported` failure. A card that marks an extension `required` that the client does not list in `supportedExtensions` is refused with `ExtensionSupportRequiredError` before any RPC. The client selects the first JSON-RPC interface that speaks A2A 1.0 on the card's own origin (or one in `allowedInterfaceOrigins`), checks the card and interface URLs against an optional egress policy, and sends `A2A-Version: 1.0` and the active extensions on every request. Without an egress policy only `https:` URLs are contacted, and redirects are never followed: a 3xx fails the call with `egress-denied`, because the checks ran on the original URL only. REST and gRPC interfaces are not selected.
- **Transport.** A card with `capabilities.streaming` is followed over `SendStreamingMessage`; a dropped stream reconnects with `SubscribeToTask`, and a stream that keeps closing without progress falls back to polling `GetTask` with doubling delays. Other cards are polled. Push notifications are not used. Retryable failures (network errors, HTTP 5xx and 429, a request that does not start answering within `requestTimeoutMs`) retry up to `attempts` times with the same `messageId`. A task that does not stop within `taskTimeoutMs` is canceled and the call fails with `timeout`.
- **Interruptions.** `input-required` and `auth-required` are distinct outcomes that leave the remote task open; sending again with its `taskId` continues it. An HTTP 401 or 403 is `unauthorized` after `authenticate` has had one chance to supply credentials for the challenge.
- **Trust.** Outcomes are marked `untrusted` and carry the peer's card name, version, URL, and task id. A `url` part is kept as a reference and never fetched, `raw` bytes are never carried, and an artifact keeps at most `artifactLimit` characters.
- **Delivery.** By default a call is synchronous within the tool call and persists nothing, so a restart does not resume it. `createPeerCallTool({ durable: bureau.peerCalls })` (COR-1670) makes it survive a restart of a durable run: the tool records the peer task id as soon as the peer accepts the message, keyed by the run and the tool call's `durableOperationKey`, and the replayed call follows that task with `A2APeerClient.attach` (a `GetTask`, then `SubscribeToTask` or polling) instead of sending again. The send carries a `messageId` derived from the operation key, so a crash between the send and the record is a repeat of the same message, which this mount answers with the task the first send created. Each record carries a digest of the request (message, `taskId`, `contextId`), because an operation key names a position in a run and a replayed step can emit other calls at the same positions: a replay whose request differs fails with `PeerCallMismatchError` naming the recorded task, with no attach and no resend. A peer that forgot the recorded task fails the replay with `task-not-found`, with no resend. A record that cannot be written cancels the task and fails the call. Records are a fourth family in the child topology store (`bureau-peer-call:record:`), deleted when the run's task is removed; a run with no task record (a child, a goal attempt, a workflow step) keeps them. The COR-51 outbox is not used.

## Changes to Weft

Weft is a public package. To make it a good base for anyone implementing A2A, not just Bureau, these changes land in Weft:

- **`@lostgradient/weft/a2a`.** A new subpath, kept free of Bun-only and UI code, built on `@a2a-js/sdk` and containing:
  - a fetch handler around the SDK's `JsonRpcTransportHandler`, with streaming over Server-Sent Events, carrying only A2A `StreamResponse` kinds, where snapshots and durable-history events carry their durable cursor as the SSE `id` (step, delta, and tool frames never reach this handler);
  - `A2A-Version` and `A2A-Extensions` negotiation;
  - the public Agent Card route, signed with the SDK's `generateAgentCardSignature`, and its public JWKS;
  - security schemes derived from Weft's `AuthConfig`;
  - durable `TaskStore` and `PushNotificationStore` implementations over Weft storage;
  - push notification delivery through Weft's outbox, sent as `Content-Type: application/a2a+json` (A2A 1.0.1 §4.3.3) and carrying the push configuration's own `authentication` (`Authorization: <scheme> <credentials>`, with credentials stored only in the push-configuration record), its `token`, when set, as the `X-A2A-Notification-Token` header (the header the pinned `@a2a-js/sdk` sender uses; A2A names none), and, when the operator provisions signing secrets, Standard Webhooks signatures in addition (HMAC-SHA256 over per-deployment secrets, COR-1471's `signingSecrets`, shared with receivers out of band and rotated by a two-secret overlap; A2A's `token` is unrelated), behind an SSRF egress policy.
- **A workflow reference backend.** Weft workflows served as A2A agents: a workflow run is a task, `waitForSignal` is `TASK_STATE_INPUT_REQUIRED`, cancellation is `CancelTask`, workflow output is artifacts, and history is the fleet feed. It is also the conformance fixture for the A2A test kit, proving the kit independent of Bureau.
- **Durable stores** over Weft storage for backends that have no task records of their own. The workflow backend uses the task store; Bureau uses only the push-configuration store, because a Bureau run already is the task.
- **Security scheme derivation.** Card `securitySchemes` generated from Weft's `AuthConfig`.
- **Foreign subscriptions over Server-Sent Events.** The generic subscription route COR-1299 adds. It is built: `createBureauHandler` serves it at `/v1/subscriptions/<name>`, and `bureau.tasks.events`, `bureau.events`, and `operative.runs.events` are read through it.
- **Fault codes.** `StaleRevision`, `RateLimited`, `Unavailable`, and `UnsupportedCapability` added to `FaultCode`.

An Agent Card's `version` is the agent's version, `definitionRevision` for Bureau and the definition's `version` for workflows, so Weft adds no server version. Rate limiting of a fetch handler, which cannot see the client address, stays with COR-718's limits criterion.

- **Package-registered scopes.** Weft's built-in scope list is closed, and a package registers its own namespaced scopes through the handler's `additionalScopes` (`packages/weft/src/server/authorization-scope.ts`). It is built: `createBureauHandler` registers `BUREAU_SCOPES` that way.

The Conversationalist-to-A2A part mapping (COR-1303) stays in Conversationalist with its own hand-written types for the pinned proto, as that issue specifies, so Conversationalist gains no dependency on Weft. A Bureau test, where both packages are available, proves that the mapping's output is accepted by `@a2a-js/sdk`'s types and request handler, so the two definitions cannot drift.

## Interborough

The first consumer is Interborough, Corvidae's main application. It is served from the same origin as the Bureau host it runs, so its calls need no CORS, and Weft's own server runs on a private loopback port behind it, so the console and the engine API answer on that one origin. A deployment that serves a UI from any other origin opts in through `createBureauHandler`'s CORS option, which must allow that origin, `GET`, `POST`, and `OPTIONS`, the request headers `Authorization`, `X-API-Key` (when the API-key scheme is configured), `Content-Type`, `A2A-Version`, `A2A-Extensions`, `X-A2A-Extensions`, `Last-Event-ID`, `If-None-Match`, and `traceparent`, and expose `A2A-Extensions`, `X-A2A-Extensions`, and `ETag`, so a cross-origin client can revalidate the Agent Card. The Interborough uses AG-UI for its live chat surface, the A2A surface for agent discovery and task lifecycle, and the management plane for operator screens. It uses the Cinder components and carries its own client provider, query helpers, router, and live-source plumbing, and it supplies its own code highlighter. It is scoped in its own Linear project; External Control Plane's Definition of Done names it as the consumer that completes discover, command, reconnect, result, abort, and cleanup without private APIs.

## Scenarios

| Scenario | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Exact retry | The original receipt and task; one run exists |
| Conflicting retry | To a client that activated command receipts, a typed conflict carrying the original receipt; to any other client, a standard `-32602` error without receipt metadata; either way the original command is untouched |
| Stale session target | The stale-revision fault; nothing changes |
| Process crash after acceptance | The retry observes the pending ledger entry and returns its outcome; no second run starts |
| Reconnect below the history floor | On A2A, the current `Task` first, with the gap in its durable-history metadata, then only events after its cursor; on `bureau.tasks.events`, one gap event, then the events from the floor |
| Slow subscriber | The durable tail is pulled at the subscriber's pace. On management streams, dropped ephemeral frames produce one frames-dropped marker; an AG-UI subscriber that falls behind is closed with `STREAM_OVERFLOW` and attaches again, and an A2A subscriber that falls behind is closed and resumes with `Last-Event-ID` |
| Resource-ownership denial | Not found, identical to a resource that does not exist |
| Attached disconnect | Not applicable in v1: every task is detached |
| Detached disconnect | The task continues; `SubscribeToTask` resumes with a snapshot |
| Cancellation race | One compare-and-swap winner. A cancel that loses to completion returns `TaskNotCancelableError` with the terminal task; a completion that loses to cancel ends `TASK_STATE_CANCELED` |
| Unknown webhook outcome | The delivery settles `unknown-outcome` and is parked for operator inspection, never silently retried as success |

## Consequences

- **COR-424** keys run creation on the caller, the opted-in agent, the A2A operation version (`A2A-Version`), and the A2A `messageId`; its receipt type becomes the ledger's projection of the receipt states above.
- **COR-718** keeps public only each Agent Card and, when the card is signed, the JWKS its signature names; registers Bureau's operator scopes; and derives the card's security declaration from enforced configuration. Its two-tenant language becomes two principals plus a two-deployment isolation check.
- **COR-841** implements A2A push notification configs on the delivery outbox, alongside the four existing triggers.
- **COR-903** implements the A2A surface as the contract, through `@lostgradient/weft/a2a`.
- **COR-905** has a named consumer: operatives calling peers.
- **COR-1298** treats the A2A adapter as the primary mount and gets its third question answered: `bureau.events` and `operative.runs.events` turn on `transports.http` with live-only semantics.
- **COR-1447** exported the shared UI layers from `@lostgradient/weft/ui`; COR-1488 moved them into `applications/interborough` when Weft's UI surface was removed.
- **COR-794** conforms to the asset section; COR-901 is no longer blocked by it. COR-1547 added the authenticated asset route and the upload operations; COR-788 projected multimodal content through the A2A surface (#527 outbound, #533 inbound).
- **COR-962** defines the interactive surface named in the taxonomy. A2UI travels to the Interborough over AG-UI and to external clients and peers over A2UI's A2A extension, which the A2A surface advertises as optional. An action from any renderer, Bureau's or a foreign host's, arrives as a command and is reauthorized like any other; the renderer is never trusted. Bureau agents emit A2UI's standard v0.9.1 basic catalog, which official renderers understand, and offer Cinder's catalog (COR-1310) only to clients that list it in `supportedCatalogIds`. The Interborough renders the basic catalog with Cinder through a renderer in `@lostgradient/chat`; Cinder gains `text`, `video-player`, and `audio-player` components and an `image` fit and size for it. Structured input requests travel as A2UI forms in the `INPUT_REQUIRED` status message, with a text fallback. The A2UI pin moves from `v0.9` to the current `v0.9.1`. COR-962 closed without shipping the contract; COR-1631 wrote it, [interactive surfaces](./interactive-surface.md), and shipped its lifecycle as `bureau.surfaces`.
- **The Weft A2A kit and its workflow reference backend** are tracked as new issues in the A2A Interoperability project, upstream of COR-903.
- **The External Control Plane and A2A Interoperability projects** drop their non-goals that kept A2A a projection, and this project's two-tenant criterion becomes two principals plus two deployments.
- **Existing issues the A2A surface depends on.** COR-1302 is a hard dependency: Bureau must mint a stable `taskId` at admission, which `bureau.tasks.start` now does: its handle carries the task's id once it resolves. `SendMessage` returns that task early only when `configuration.returnImmediately` is true; otherwise it waits, as A2A 1.0.1 §3.2.2 requires, until the task is terminal or interrupted. COR-1301 (descriptions and skills), COR-1303 (message-to-part mapping, in Conversationalist), COR-1304 (caller-scoped task listing), and COR-855 (the AG-UI projection) supply the rest.
- **New work to scope.** No existing issue owned these when the document was written, so each needed scoping before the surface that relies on it shipped. Most of them have shipped since, and [As built](#as-built) says which surface exists; the A2A items remain decisions:
  - the Weft A2A kit, its workflow reference backend (both in A2A Interoperability), and the Weft changes listed above, including package-registered scopes and the new fault codes;
  - delivering `SendMessage` with a `taskId` to a live task, which needs session-input admission (served by `bureau.contexts.input` through the session mailbox, delivered to the live run at its next step boundary by COR-1476, and outside COR-424's scope);
  - durable-history maintenance: advancing the retention floor, replacing the silent gap skip with the explicit gap event, the frames-dropped marker (management streams only: an AG-UI stream closes on overflow instead), and deleting terminal tasks after `retention.tasks`;
  - the management operations COR-1300 and COR-841 do not cover: `bureau.deployment.get`, `bureau.agents.list`, `bureau.tasks.*`, `bureau.contexts.delete`, `bureau.contexts.steer`, `bureau.contexts.fork`, `bureau.contexts.rename`, `bureau.agents.models`, `bureau.audit.query`, `bureau.decisions.decide`, `bureau.settings.*`, `bureau.schedules.*`, `bureau.goals.*`, and `bureau.memory.*`;
  - the Interborough project.
